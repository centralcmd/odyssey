Contract term direction · Outgoing · Incoming

A priced term used to have no way of saying which way the money moves, so every term was implicitly a cost: a landlord's rent summed into the same “what this file costs to run” figure as a streaming subscription, and a salary read as an expense. Every contract term now carries a required Direction — Outgoing or Incoming, stated from the household's perspective, never from either named party's — and the contracts header reports what goes out, what comes in and an explicit net instead of one figure that quietly mixed them. Nothing changes for a household that only records costs: every existing term is Outgoing, and every figure keeps its exact value.

  • sellDirection is a property of the term, not the contract. An employment agreement pays a salary in and deducts dues and a pension contribution out; a tenancy where the household is landlord takes rent in and pays a management fee out. One flag on the contract could express neither, so no surface offers a contract-level direction.
  • addThe magnitude stays positive; the direction carries the sign. Income is never entered as a negative amount — that would make the sign mean two different things depending on who typed it, and would break the gross split the moment both conventions appeared in one file. The dialog's helper says so on both settings.
  • historyCorrecting a direction supersedes; it never forks. Direction is on the entry, not the series, so it is not part of (owner, kind, label): a mis-directed term is an ordinary replace, the history stays one series, and the direction that counts is the one on the entry in force.
  • badgeThe party role is a hint at most, never the source. Employee / Landlord / Seller look like they imply a direction, but nothing in the model says which party is the household — and a derivation that guessed wrong would put money on the wrong side of the net, silently. The role may pre-select the control; it never overrides it.
  • functionsNo gross ever mixes the two sides. One unconverted-currency list covers both: a currency with no rate to base is named and excluded from each gross and from the net — never folded in at 1:1 — so the net is partial exactly when the grosses are.
  • visibility_offThe list row's marker is a frontend ask, not a field that exists. “Money in” on a collapsed record is derived from that record's own in-force terms. The list projection carries no direction (it has no term join), so shipping this marker needs either the detail payload the expanded card already holds or a list-level field the backend deliberately deferred.
