A3 · the countdown rail

A3 puts distance in the rail instead of a date: the number is how many days, the calendar date drops to a caption. That trade only works if the number holds up across the whole range a task can sit in — today, tomorrow, four months out, a year overdue — so these five variants argue about the number's format and tone, and the last section runs every one of them through the full scale.

A3.1Signed number

Round two's card, tightened: the count set in the mono face with a leading sign, so overdue and remaining are the same glyph pattern and the column of numbers aligns down the list. "DAYS" stops being repeated in two wordings — the sign carries the direction.

A3.3Tinted field

A3.1's numbers with the urgency wash filling the rail. The countdown becomes a colour-coded gutter you can scan without reading — and unlike the date rail, a tinted countdown column never looks like a calendar, so the tint has nothing to fight. Fine at one or two per screen; a fully-overdue list is loud.

A3.4Ring

The count sits inside an arc showing how much of the task's own window — created date to deadline — has burned. Two facts in the space of one: how long is left, and whether that's most or a sliver of the time allowed. An overdue task fills the ring completely. The arc is decoration on any task created the same day it's due, which is a fair share of them.

Every rail state a task can produce, at A3.1's format. Reading

A3.5 on A3.1's signed format, with three rules from the scale: TODAY and TMRW as words (a bare "0" reads as absent), days collapsing to months past 90, and a tick with the completion date once a task is Done — the "2 days early" variant is a nice fact but it makes a finished task look like it still has a clock. The ring in A3.4 is the one I'd hold back: it needs a created-to-due window that half the seeded tasks don't really have.
