Task list card — three directions

Today a task in /tasks list view borrows the finance record card (.acct-item): a collapsed row whose author, completion time and note are crushed into one dot-separated subline, with nothing to expand into. These three take the journal entry card's premise instead — the card is always open and is the detail — and argue about what earns the left rail.

A · Deadline rail

The journal card's date leaf, repointed at the thing a task is actually anchored to: its deadline. The rail carries day-of-week, date and the relative count, tinted by urgency (overdue red, ≤3 days pending amber), so a scan down the list reads as a calendar column. Tasks with no deadline show an em dash — the empty rail is itself information. Status stays a circle in the title row; the note is never truncated.

B · Status rail

The rail carries state instead of time: the cycling circle at the top with the status word set vertically beneath it, so the list has a colour-coded spine and the status control lands in the same place on every card. The deadline returns to the title row as the chip it already is. Note clamped to two lines here — the card is a row, not a document.

Where they differ

A gives the deadline a fixed, scannable slot and reads best on a long overdue-heavy list, at the cost of ~92px of width and a rail that's empty for undated tasks. B makes status the spine — best if the list is mostly a triage queue — but pushes the deadline into the chip row where it competes with tags. C is the least disruptive and densest, and the only one whose rhythm still matches the finance record cards above it in the module. My pick is A, with B's vertical status word dropped: the deadline is the only thing in a task worth a column of its own.
