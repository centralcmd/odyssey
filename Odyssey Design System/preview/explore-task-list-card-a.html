A · deadline rail — six variants

All six keep A's premise: the left rail belongs to the deadline, the card is always open, the note is never truncated. What's in play is how loud urgency gets, what the rail shows when there's nothing to show, where the status control lives, and how dense the whole row should be. Each variant repeats the same four tasks so they can be compared line for line.

A1Text-tinted rail

Round one's card, unchanged. Only the month and the relative count take the urgency colour; the rail's field stays the neutral grey the journal card uses. Quietest option — urgency reads only once you look at the rail.

A3Countdown hero

A4Status in the rail

The cycling circle moves into the rail above the date, which frees the title row entirely — the title starts at the body's padding edge and nothing below it needs indenting. Status and deadline become one control column. Costs the title row its status colour anchor, and the circle is further from the title it belongs to.

The same card at list density: 74px rail, no day-of-week, body1 title, tighter gaps. About 30% shorter per row, so a screen holds five or six tasks instead of three. The question is whether /tasks is a reading surface or a working queue — this is the queue answer, and it's also the closest in height to today's .acct-item row.

A6Rail repoints by status

The rail always shows the date that matters now, labelled: DUE while the task is open, DONE once it's completed, ARCHIVED after. A finished task stops advertising a deadline it no longer has, and the completion time leaves the meta line. Adds a label line to every rail — the price of never being ambiguous about which date you're reading.

Reading

A5 with A2's tint is my read: the compact rail keeps /tasks a working list rather than a reading list, and at that size the tinted field is what makes urgency survive the smaller type. A6's labelled rail is the one idea worth folding in regardless — a Done card showing a deadline is the current row's worst ambiguity. For the undated case, U1 with A5's compact rail; U4 is tempting but puts a second click target in a column that otherwise never takes clicks.
