Contract party roles and the type × role matrix

What a linked record does in an agreement. The vocabulary is fifteen live members: the four generic ones that survived, plus Landlord / Tenant, the four insurance roles, Lender / Borrower for the new Loan type, and Guarantor / Broker. Unspecified (0) and ServiceProvider (5) are retired — a role is now required on every party write, and supplying a service is what Seller already meant. Their ordinals stay permanent holes: reusing one would make an unmigrated row mean something new rather than nothing. Which roles are legal is decided per contract type by one shared matrix, so the picker and the server validator name the same declaration instead of the client keeping a copy of a server rule.

  • ruleSuggested carries no server meaning. It is the ordering, declared once beside the legality it has to stay consistent with. Every type has at least one suggested role, so the picker's first group is never empty — including Other-the-type, which suggests Other-the-role because a contract that is "none of the above" has no vocabulary to offer.
  • shieldInsurance is the only type with four suggested roles, mirroring the four link collections on an insurance policy. Insured is one member for both party kinds — the kind discriminator already says whether the covered thing is an account or a contact.
  • handshakeBroker and Other are legal on every type, and Guarantor on the three where somebody stands behind an obligation: Rental, Purchase and Loan.
  • volunteer_activismBeneficiary blocks contact deletion. A contact named as a beneficiary on a contract refuses the ordinary delete with a 409, the same way a policy beneficiary does, and clears through the same one-transaction detach valve.
  • paletteColours sit in the shared categorical band (L ~0.75–0.80, C ~0.13). Broker is deliberately low-chroma: it is legal everywhere and should not read as a category of its own.
