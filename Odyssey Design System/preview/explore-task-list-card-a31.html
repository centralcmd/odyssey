A3.1 · the rail is the count

The rail now holds one thing and holds it at the top: how many days. The calendar date moves into the card, where it can be labelled and read as text, and the status control drops into the rail below the number — so the left column becomes "how urgent, and what state", and the body carries everything you'd read a sentence of. Five arrangements of that rail, then a study of where the date should actually land in the body.

B1Divided rail

Count top-aligned, a hairline beneath it, then the status circle. The divider says the two halves are different facts rather than one stacked group, and it gives the circle a fixed position no matter how tall the card grows. Date sits first in the body's meta line, labelled "Due".

B2Count top, control at the foot

B3Labelled control

B1 without the hairline, and the status control gains its own word underneath — so the rail reads top to bottom as "−8 days · Backlog". The state is then text as well as colour, which is the accessibility answer the board chips already give; it costs a line and makes the rail busier than a single number deserves.

Reading

B1's divider is what makes the arrangement work — without it (B2) the circle drifts with card height, and with a label (B3) the rail starts competing with the title for attention. For the date, D1: the meta line is already the card's line of quiet facts, and "Due 9 Sep 2026" as text can say due, which a chip can't. D3 is the fallback if keeping the shipped DeadlineChip matters more than the tidier line — but then the same urgency is stated three times on one card.
