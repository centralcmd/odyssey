Journal entry card — four directions

All four break from the shared RecordCard row: the entry text is readable without expanding, photos carry weight, and the meta line is no longer a run of tiny count icons. A and D are always-open (no chevron); B stays collapsible; C drops the card box entirely. Same content in each, so they compare directly.

A Datebook

Date rail, open by default

The date becomes the anchor instead of a mono string in the meta line — a calendar-leaf rail down the left edge. Full text, a photo strip, and one footer line of tags and contacts. Nothing to expand; the card is the entry.

B Photo-led

Photo band on top, still collapsible

Photos are the card's first impression: a full-bleed band, hero plus two, overflow counted in the corner. Below it a two-line snippet so the list still scans, and expand for the full text, attachments and links. Entries without photos drop the band and read as a compact text card.

C Timeline

No card at all — entries on a spine

The box goes away. A dated spine carries the entries, each one a heading, its text, a thumbnail strip and a quiet meta line. The least chrome of the four and the furthest from a ledger row; the tradeoff is weaker row boundaries and no per-entry surface to hang an expanded detail on.

D Page

Entry as a page, metadata in a margin

The text is the card: larger type, generous measure, nothing clamped. Every field the expanded detail used to reveal — date, author, location, tags, contacts, attachments — sits in a right-hand margin column, so there is no expand step and no nested tile grid. The heaviest of the four per row; best paired with a smaller default page size.
