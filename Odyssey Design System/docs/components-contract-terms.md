# Components — Contract terms

> Part of the [Odyssey Design System](../README.md) docs. Foundations, tokens and the component catalog live in the README; this file is the per-feature detail.


The **Terms** section (`ContractTerms.jsx`) is a zone inside the expanded contract record, **between Parties and Documents** — who is in the agreement, what it costs, what evidences it. It renders as **two sibling sections**, `Current terms` and `Term history`, at the same level as Parties and Documents (what the contract costs today and how it got there are separate questions); a contract with no terms collapses to one `Terms` section carrying the empty state. Reference build: `ui_kits/web/ContractTerms.jsx` + `ui_kits/web/AddContractTermModal.jsx` (seed + helpers in `contracts-data.js`, styles `.con-trm-*` in `contracts.css`); specimens: `preview/31b-contract-terms.html`, dialog `preview/48b-dialog-new-contract-term.html`.

**It is the account rate & fee surface, owned by a contract.** A term is the *same* `Term` row, in the same table, under the same rules — the series key is `(owner, kind, label)`, the latest entry on or before a date is the value **in force**, supersession is implicit, and a row hangs off **exactly one** of `AccountId` / `ContractId`. The in-force values render as the record's own **`InfoTile`s**, exactly as the account record's *Current* section renders its terms, and the history is `TermHistory` unchanged — with the in-force / superseded / **Scheduled** wording and the cadence phrasing. Two copies of these would drift; the owner-specific facts arrive as props.

**What it changes, and what it adds.** The section leads with *Current terms*, then the history. The account surface's step chart comes across, but **not as a hero and not as a rate**: an agreement carries several named charges and no single line stands for it, so the chart sits **above the history as a multi-select chooser** — opening on the series whose latest entry is the most recent, and letting the reader add up to four charges measured the same way. Comparing plots **indexed change** from each charge's own first entry by default — a 2,250 rent and a 95 parking space share no useful absolute axis — with a **Change / Value** toggle handing that choice to the reader and the legend keeping the real money either way. Charges in another currency or unit switch rather than stack. The plot is the DS **`StepChart`**, so the card, head and typography are the dashboard's chart vocabulary while the time axis, staircase and today marker stay what a price history needs. Three rules are contract-specific and each is *drawn*, not assumed:

- **Kind eligibility** — **Fee** and **Interest rate** on every `ContractType`; `ExpectedReturn` is refused, and the dialog says why rather than silently omitting it. There is no per-type matrix.
- **Currency is required, and prefilled from the user's default** — an amount term **requires** an explicit `currencyCode`, and the dialog opens on **Preferences → Default currency** (falling back to the workspace base) because a contract has no currency of its own to lend. The preference is a default, not an assumption: clearing the field still refuses the write, and a term priced elsewhere is one pick away. Originally the field opened unset; An account lends its own currency; a contract has none, so the field opens unset with a helper that says so, and submitting without it is refused inline (the `400`).
- **Archived changes nothing here** — an archived contract still records and edits terms, and keeps its per-row controls. Archival hides the contract from the default list; the final rent or closing fee is exactly what gets recorded after an agreement ends. The cap is the only thing that refuses a term write.

**The cap is stated where the writing happens.** At `ContractMaxTermsPerContract` (ships at 500), the record menu's **New term** is disabled and names the limit — the `422` — while editing an existing entry stays available, because a `PUT` replaces a row rather than adding one. The kit exposes the cap as a *Max terms per contract* tweak so the refusal is a state you can see.

**On the collapsed card**, the counts strip gains a **Terms** count beside Parties and Documents — term **rows**, not in-force series, counted in the one list query alongside `PartyCount` / `FileCount`.

**Creating never names the owner.** The dialog posts a `NewTerm` with no owner id in it; the route is the only thing that says which contract, so a `contracts.update` holder cannot write a term onto an account. Re-parenting a term is a delete plus a create.

> **Stack reality check.** Mirrors the *Contract Terms* backend (Draft v2): the existing `Terms` table widened to two owners (`AccountId` nullable, new nullable `ContractId`, a `CK_Terms_ExactlyOneOwner` check, a mirrored index), one extended `TermService` for both owners, and five contract-scoped routes on `ContractController` — `GET …/terms`, `GET …/terms/current`, `POST`, `PUT`, `DELETE` — gated on the **existing** `contracts.read` / `contracts.update` (no new claim, so no sign-out on deploy). `GET /api/contracts/{id}` gains `currentTerms`, the list row gains `termCount`, and `ContractMaxTermsPerContract` is a `SystemSettings` row. Single-term operations filter on the `(ContractId, TermId)` pair, so a term owned by an account or another contract is a `404`, never an existence oracle.
