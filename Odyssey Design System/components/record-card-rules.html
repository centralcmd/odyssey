RecordCard — content rules

Every record list in the product uses one card: Accounts, Contracts, Insurance policies, Subscriptions, Tax statements, Budgets, Journal entries, Exchange rates. The shell is RecordCard; these rules govern what goes in it. They come out of the insurance policy card — the one page whose expanded body read as a record rather than a wall of wells, because its facts are small labelled tiles instead of two columns of large ones. The card is the product’s only record-detail surface — the pattern was weighed against modals, drawers and per-record pages, and expandable cards won.

Body order · fixed by the component, not by the page
alertThe problem or warning that is the reason the card was opened. Always first.
detailsThe facts, as an InfoTileGrid of InfoTiles.
contentDescription, notes or entry text — one wide tile.
sectionsSub-collections, each under a SectionDivider, in the header's count order.
The seventeen rules
  1. The header is the record's identity. It carries enough to recognise, compare and rule out a record without expanding it: type mark, name, one state chip, a problem chip if there is one, the meta line, the headline figure, the counts, the actions. Density is intended — but one line, ellipsised, never wrapped.
  2. Body order is fixed on every record type: alert, details, content, sections. A record may omit any of them; none may be reordered.
  3. The details show the record’s full field set — including the fields the header already shows. At tile scale a value arrives with its own label, so a labelled Billing interval tile and the header’s meta line read as two different things: the header is for scanning a list, the body is the record. A reader should not have to reassemble a record from a header plus a remainder.
  4. A field with no value shows no tile. Empty state is not a fact, and a grid of “Not set” wastes the reader’s attention on absences. Keep an empty tile only where the absence itself is the fact and a reader would otherwise wonder — a subscription with no end date is open-ended; a policy with no insurer is broken. That is a per-field decision, made once, with the reason written down; it is never the default.
  5. Tiles never condition on each other. A tile renders if and only if its own field has a value — never because another tile "already shows that". Deferring between tiles reads as tidy and fails silently: the tile it defers to resolves one state by precedence, so the moment a second state is set the deferred fact vanishes with no error and no empty slot to notice. Some overlap with a derived summary is the price of that guarantee, and at tile scale it is a small price.
  6. A derived tile summarises; it does not replace. Status, coverage, review state and the like are computed from other fields — they carry the state and the date that state began, and the fields they are computed from still render their own tiles. A status is a reading of the record, not a home for the record’s data.
  7. Foot captions add precision, they are not a toll. Where a field has something more to say — what the currency converts to, where the custodian is, how many days until the next billing — it goes in the foot. Where it does not, the tile stands with label and value alone. Never write a foot to justify a tile.
  8. No tile ceiling — completeness beats brevity here. A record with twelve scalar fields shows twelve tiles; the auto-fitting grid absorbs them three or four to a row. Facts are never offloaded to the edit dialog, which keeps every editable field regardless of what the card shows.
  9. The iconed grid is the default at any count. InfoTileGrid dense — no icon chips, tiles fitting at minmax(152px) — is an option for record types whose facts are many and short (tax statements). Decided per record type, never mixed inside one card.
  10. Prose is not a field. Description, notes and entry text go in one wide tile in the content slot — never squeezed into a tile column, never as a bare paragraph.
  11. Sections come last, in the header's count order. A section that only presents content is introduced by a SectionDivider — uppercase label, hairline rule, mono meta. A section whose sub-collection is optional (estimates, terms — the ones the header only counts when non-zero) is omitted entirely when empty, matching the empty-field rule. Two kinds of section stay at zero: one intrinsic to the record (its files, its transactions), where the absence is a fact worth stating; and one that is the only entry point for creating its first item (smart tags, whose adder lives in the section and has no row-menu equivalent) — hiding it would make the feature unreachable. Sections are not collapsible and carry no action slot: a body already opens as a whole, and every section-level action ("New period", "Attach document") belongs to the row action menu, where the reader looks for actions on this record. A section header labels; it does not act. A record with a current state ("Current terms", "Current period") puts that band first, above the sub-collections.
  12. One accent per record, taken from its type — or its type-equivalent. The header mark shows that glyph in that colour, and every other icon in the card plus any single-series chart uses the same colour. A type-equivalent is a categorical registry the record always has exactly one of, carrying its own icon + colour: the billing interval on a subscription, the category on a budget item. A cadence is not a status — derived state (paused, ended, overdue) is never the accent; it stays with the status chip. A record with neither a type nor an equivalent falls back to the brand accent. The accent covers what belongs to this record: its mark, its tile chips, its charts.
  13. A reference to another object is drawn in that object’s own icon and colour. An attached account, a linked contact, an insurance policy, a file type — each keeps the mark it carries on its own page, taken from its own type. It is a pointer somewhere else, not a fact about this record, and it should be recognisable as the same thing the reader saw in its own list. So a card may legitimately show several hues: one is the record’s own, the rest belong to the records it points at.
  14. One card open at a time. Opening a record closes its siblings, so the list never holds two tall bodies at once and the record below stays reachable.
  15. The card is the only detail surface — there are no record pages. Sections therefore carry whatever the record needs: charts, paged tables, inline edit, their own dialogs. There is no height budget; the discipline is the full field set and the foot rule (nothing restated for free) plus one card open at a time — not a cap on what a section may contain.
  16. One row height across every record list — at least 88px. The component owns it; a page never tightens or pads its rows to suit its own content. Insurance sets the reference, because its meta line carries linked-record pill chips and those are worth the height; a list of plainer records keeps the same rhythm so the product scans as one thing. A record needing more room wraps to a taller row — it never drops below the shared floor.
  17. Header counts are the body's table of contents. Same glyphs, same order, one section per count — and the counts stay live while the body edits them.

Mechanically it is two custom properties on the card — --rec and --rec-soft, set from accent / accentSoft — so nothing downstream needs to know the colour. InfoTileGrid maps them onto the InfoTile accent, and a chart inside the body should read var(--rec) for its single series. Semantic colour is unaffected: income stays mint, expense stays coral, status chips keep their tones.

Settled
check_circleTiles are independent of each other. Chosen over per-case suppression after two silent data losses in the pilot — an end date and a paused timestamp both disappeared when a second state took precedence in the Status tile. Correctness beats the small overlap.
check_circleRepetition between header and body is fine. Reversed after the tile treatment landed: small labelled tiles make a repeated value read as a field, not an echo, so the body carries the complete record.
check_circleThe heavy sections stay in the card. Estimates charts, term history, smart-tag tables and the file-analysis flow keep living in the body. There are no record pages, so nothing moves out.
check_circleOne card open at a time. Opening a record closes its siblings — which is what makes an unbounded body height acceptable.
check_circleThe edit dialogs stay as they are. No fields move into them and none move out; the card decides what it shows, the dialog keeps everything editable.
check_circleType colours are complete. Audited: ACCOUNT_TYPES, CONTRACT_TYPES, INSURANCE_POLICY_TYPES, CONTACT_TYPES and BUDGET_CATEGORY_TYPES all carry color + soft, so the accent rule can be trusted. Subscriptions have no type field but do have a type-equivalent — BILLING_INTERVALS, which carries icon + colour — and use it. Tax statements, journal entries and exchange rates have neither and take the brand accent.
